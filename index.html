ஐந்திணைAintinai

A landscape is also
a way of feeling.

In classical Tamil Sangam poetry, the five landscapes connect the natural world with our inner lives. Mountains speak of union. Forests, of waiting. The coast, of longing.

I made Aintinai to bring those worlds to life, drawing on the colours, dark outlines, and ornamental borders of Nayak temple murals. Its paths eventually lead to Madurai, where I’m from.

A little about me

I build learning systems that connect perception, prediction, and action on physical robots.

I’m a Member of Technical Staff at a stealth company, working on world action models and industrial robot manipulation. My work spans world models, robot foundation models, multimodal learning, and 3D perception, with a focus on reusable manipulation skills, online policy refinement, and reliable real-world deployment.

I completed my master’s at the University of Michigan in May 2026, where I worked with Prof. Katie Skinner in the Field Robotics Group on underwater perception and SLAM.

Outside robotics, I make generative art rooted in Tamil landscapes and the streets of Madurai. I also write on Latent Gaussian.

SCOUT

Submitted to ICLR 2027

Critic-Guided Schrödinger Transport for Online VLA Refinement

Sacchin Sundar1*, Huaidian Hou1*, Yaoru Liu2, Dong Sun2, Siyuan Ji2, Junshan Zhang1,3 (*equal contribution)

Read abstract

Critic-guided Schrödinger bridge refinement adapts frozen vision-language-action (VLA) policies using sparse rewards. A lightweight two-step actor removes candidate search and critic evaluation from inference. Evaluated on LIBERO, LIBERO-PRO, and bimanual robot hardware, with 92.3% mean LIBERO success compared with a 53.1% base policy, and stacking time reduced from 26.1 to 13.3 seconds.

SurfSLAM

Accepted to IEEE Transactions on Field Robotics

Sim-to-Real Underwater Stereo Reconstruction For Real-Time SLAM

Onur Bagoren*, Seth Isaacson*, Sacchin Sundar, Yung-Ching Sun, Anja Sheppard, Haoyu Ma, Abrar Shariff, Ram Vasudevan, Katherine A. Skinner (*equal contribution)

Read abstract

SurfSLAM is a real-time underwater SLAM framework that combines learned stereo depth with acoustic-inertial odometry for localization and dense 3D reconstruction. Its sim-to-real training pipeline uses physics-based underwater image augmentation and self-supervised fine-tuning to adapt stereo networks to challenging underwater conditions. Evaluated on real-world shipwreck surveys, the framework improves stereo depth estimation, trajectory estimation, and reconstruction of complex underwater scenes.
